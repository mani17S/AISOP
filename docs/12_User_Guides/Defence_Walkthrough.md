## 2. The five criticisms: honest answers

### 2.1 Lack of research novelty
- Honest answer: novelty is unproven. The project has not done a literature or product review (`docs/01_Project_Foundation/Research_Gap.md` says so in its first paragraph). Several ideas are adapted from public work, acknowledged in `docs/10_Research/AiSOC_Adaptation_Register.md` (the link-strength tiers come from AiSOC). Present it as an applied integration with a testable hypothesis.
- The hypothesis (H1, `docs/10_Research/Evaluation.md`): on fixed synthetic scenarios, a gate that treats duplicates and weak links as non-corroborating and abstains on thin evidence gives a lower false-sufficient rate and lower unsupported-claim rate than a naive baseline (B0: any two records sharing an entity corroborate; B1: B0 plus exact-duplicate removal). It also lists what would falsify it.
- Evidence you can show: baselines implemented as pure functions with tests (`backend/app/modules/evaluation/baselines.py`); on the development split B0 says SUFFICIENT for 29 of 67, B1 for 24, AISOP for 11. Dataset in `datasets/sufficiency_eval` with hash manifest `MANIFEST.sha256`; scorer `backend/scripts/score_sufficiency_baselines.py`.
- NOT yet proven: no owner labels (`labels.json` not written), so no accuracy, false-sufficient rate or agreement figure; held-out split never run; no second reviewer; the author wrote both the rules and (would write) the labels; no literature comparison; no real data.
- Say: "This is an applied integration of known ideas. My contribution is making independence and abstention explicit, deterministic and testable in one local tool. Whether that is novel I cannot yet say, because I have not completed the literature review. Here is the hypothesis and what would falsify it."
- Avoid: "novel", "first", "state of the art", "outperforms", "proven", "research gap established", any citation you have not read. The phase 1 reference deck cites papers that were not verified (`Research_Gap.md`), so do not repeat them.
- Best use of the remaining days: write the labels for the 67 development cases, run the scorer, and report the result whatever it is, including failures.

### 2.2 Weak SOC architecture understanding
- Honest answer: the architecture is documented and drawn from the source, with each component marked IMPLEMENTED, PARTIAL or PLANNED (`docs/03_System_Design/Architecture_Diagrams.md`). Be able to say where AISOP sits in a SOC: it is an investigation and evidence-qualification layer after detection. It does not detect, and it takes no response action.
- Evidence: ADR-007 (vendor-neutral ingestion, the standards OCSF, ECS, ASIM and STIX evaluated and what was adopted from each), ADR-008 (sufficiency gate), ADR-009 (correlation and ATT&CK), ADR-010 (case-scoped API), ADR-011 (source-agnostic evidence domains), ADR-013 (contradiction-aware sufficiency, approved for implementation). Show the sequence diagram and trust-boundary diagram.
- Status caveat to state: `docs/03_System_Design/ADR/README.md` lists ADR-008 to ADR-011 as PROPOSED with approval pending, ADR-007 and ADR-013 as approved, and ADR-012 (Detection Engineering) as design only. Do not say "all ADRs approved".
- NOT proven: a SOC analyst's judgement of usefulness (no user study); fit with a real SIEM/SOAR deployment; streaming or high volume (batch only, one node, correlation is quadratic in incident-sized batches).
- Be ready to name SOC concepts without notes: telemetry vs alert vs evidence vs investigation (ADR-007 section 3), tiers (the roles `analyst_tier_1`, case lead), kill chain and ATT&CK, triage, corroboration, chain of custody (SHA-256 and the hash-chained ledger).
- Avoid: "replaces a SIEM", "SOAR", "detects attacks", "enterprise-grade", "full SOC platform".

### 2.3 Limited alert ingestion (previously only CrowdStrike)
- Honest answer: ingestion is now multi-source in structure, but verified on sample-shaped synthetic records only.
- Evidence: 8 adapters registered in `get_default_registry()` (`backend/app/modules/alerts/adapters/`: Sysmon, Windows Security, Linux auth, EDR, network IDS, cloud audit, email phishing, generic SIEM). Sample inputs for each in `datasets/samples/alerts/` and `datasets/samples/sysmon/` (9 Sysmon event types). 6 domain adapters plus a Suricata EVE converter (`evidence_domains/suricata_eve.py`, `backend/scripts/eve_to_envelopes.py`). Endpoint `POST /api/v1/alerts/ingest`, listing at `GET /api/v1/alerts/adapters`. Batch limit 1,000 alerts per request (`backend/app/schemas/alert_ingest.py: MAX_ALERT_BATCH_SIZE`). Duplicates skipped by SHA-256. A 200-alert batch took about 2 s (saved log). Mixed-source correlation is shown in scenario `01_intrusion_chain` of `backend/app/modules/correlation/demo_data.py`.
- NOT proven: that the adapters parse real vendor exports. The EDR adapter mentions CrowdStrike, Defender and SentinelOne, but the records tested are samples written for this project. The Architecture diagram says "sample-shaped". Suricata conversion covers alert, dns and flow only and was synthetic-tested.
- Say: "CrowdStrike was the first source. The design is now an adapter registry behind one canonical schema, with eight source types tested on synthetic samples. Adding a source means adding an adapter. I have not validated against real vendor exports."
- Avoid: "supports Splunk, Sentinel, CrowdStrike, Suricata" as integrations, "multi-vendor verified", "real-time", "any log format".

- NOT proven: authorship per file. I could not verify who wrote what. Also, tests written with AI assistance are not an independent check on AI-written code, so say tests are evidence of behaviour, not proof of correctness.
- Say: "I used AI coding assistants under written rules in `CLAUDE.md`. I do not claim to have typed every line. I claim that I specified the design, the decisions are in the ADRs, the behaviour is covered by tests, and I can explain and change the core logic. Ask me to walk through any of these files."
- Avoid: "I wrote everything by hand", "no AI was used", "the AI did it, I don't know how it works", "the tests prove it is correct".

### 2.5 Crash when handling multiple alerts
- Honest answer: the original crash was never reproduced, so you cannot say it is fixed. What you can say is what was found, measured and tested.
- Evidence: a reliability matrix R01 to R12 covering single alert, sequential multi-source, concurrent ingest, concurrent duplicates, concurrent investigations on separate cases, malformed input, large payloads, commit failure, LLM timeout, retry bounds (`backend/tests/unit/test_reliability_matrix.py`, 25 test functions; offline in the unit suite; 38 passed on PostgreSQL per saved log, 3 not executable live). Race tests for duplicate inserts (`backend/tests/unit/test_stage6a_hardening.py`: a lost insert race is an ordinary duplicate, not a 500; `test_ingestion_service.py::test_concurrent_duplicate_savepoint_handling`). Limits: 1,000 alerts per batch, 1,000 evidence items per investigation, a per-case lock and a cap of 5 model calls per run (`multisource_investigation_service.py`, `correlation/workflow.py: MAX_LLM_INCIDENTS`). A measured cause of long stalls: on Windows, `localhost` resolved to IPv6 first and new database connections stalled up to the 60 s timeout, where `127.0.0.1` took at most 0.13 s for eight simultaneous connections (`docs/11_Deployment/Local_Setup.md`, commit 97f9070). A separate incident where the backend stopped silently had no proven cause, probably external termination (`docs/11_Deployment/Demo_Runbook.md` section 5); a supervisor script restarts it and logs the exit code.
- NOT proven: that the panel's crash had that cause; behaviour under real concurrent load (the offline matrix uses a cooperative single-session harness, as the test file itself states); true multi-connection row-lock contention (noted as needing PostgreSQL verification in `test_case_api.py`).
- Say: "I could not reproduce the crash you saw. I built a 12-case reliability matrix for multiple alerts, found and fixed one measurable stall caused by `localhost` resolving to IPv6, and the matrix passes on PostgreSQL. I do not claim the original crash is fixed."
- Avoid: "the crash is fixed", "handles any load", "production scale", "thread-safe".

## 3. Question bank (15)

1. **Why not just use Splunk or Sentinel?** Those are detection and SIEM platforms; AISOP is not trying to replace them. It sits after them: it takes their alerts as input (there is a generic SIEM adapter) and checks whether the combined evidence supports a conclusion, with duplicates and weak links not counted as corroboration, and abstains when it does not. I have not benchmarked against any commercial tool and do not claim to be better. Show: `datasets/samples/alerts/generic_siem_correlation_alert.json`, ADR-007 section 3.
2. **What happens when the LLM lies?** The model is never a source of evidence. Its output is parsed, each claim must cite evidence ids from the current case, and `claim_verifier.py` checks structured assertions against the evidence without asking a model. Unsupported claims are demoted, invalid output falls back to the deterministic report. Show: `backend/tests/unit/test_claim_verifier.py`, the hostile-output test in `test_stage5_adversarial.py`. Limit: only structured assertions are checked, not prose, and real-model prompt-injection testing was not done (scripted providers only).
3. **How do you know the gate is right when there are no labels?** I do not yet. Today the gate is checked for behaviour (fixed rules, adversarial unit tests, label-independent counts 11/52/4 on the development split, all evidence references valid 67 of 67), not accuracy. The accuracy experiment needs labels that are not written yet. Show: `docs/10_Research/Evaluation.md` status table. Say this directly.
4. **What is real versus synthetic data?** All of it is synthetic and author-built: the 8 alert samples, the demo scenarios, the 20 ground-truth scenarios and the 115 derived cases. Only the ATT&CK knowledge file (pinned v20.1, SHA-256 checked) and the two public threat-intelligence feed files are external, and the feeds are not applied inside investigations. Show: `docs/03_System_Design/Architecture_Diagrams.md` header, `docs/11_Deployment/Local_Setup.md` sections 4 and 5.
5. **What is your contribution beyond calling an LLM?** Almost all of the logic is deterministic and works with the model off: ingestion, correlation, independence rules, ATT&CK rules, sufficiency gate, report. The LLM is an optional last step behind the gate. Whether the rules are better than naive correlation is hypothesis H1, not yet tested. Show: `backend/app/modules/correlation/workflow.py: analyze` versus `run_workflow`.
6. **What exactly counts as independent corroboration?** A different raw record (attested digest) that is not a duplicate or near-duplicate, of a different observation class, joined to the anchor by a strong link (logon session, process GUID) or specific link (file hash, domain, public IP, URL, e-mail). Host, user, process name and internal IPs are weak and never corroborate. Placeholder values like `unknown` or `localhost` are weak. Show: `backend/app/modules/evidence/independence.py`, ADR-008 section 3, `test_evidence_independence.py`.
7. **What stops an attacker from forging corroboration?** Duplicates and replays do not count, and hostile text never changes the verdict. A compromised source that fabricates two different kinds of event sharing a specific artefact can still obtain SUFFICIENT. This is a known limit (R1 in ADR-008, requiring sensor-family distinction, which is PROPOSED). Show: ADR-008 Stage 5 section.
8. **Is the verdict a probability or confidence score?** No. It is a rule outcome with reason codes. No numeric score and no tuned threshold, so each verdict can be explained. Show: `sufficiency_gate.py: assess_evidence_sufficiency`, ADR-008 section 5 (alternative B rejected).
9. **What if two sources disagree?** A material contradiction caps the verdict at LIMITED with `CONTRADICTORY_EVIDENCE:<kind>`, removes the conflicting pair from corroboration, and is shown in the report. Implemented kinds: ordering and outcome, plus identity and temporal per the ADR index. State-based contradictions are not declared. Retry after failure is treated as non-contradictory. Show: `backend/app/modules/evidence_domains/contradictions.py`, ADR-013, demo scenario 03.
10. **How are ATT&CK mappings produced, and can they be wrong?** From 13 named rules, each requiring specific evidence, each with a stated limitation. The LLM never maps techniques. They can be wrong or over-broad (a Run key can be benign), which is why each mapping shows its limitation and the rule id. Coverage is small: 13 techniques, not the whole matrix. Show: `correlation/mitre.py`, `evidence_domains/domain_attack.py`, the attack section in the UI.
11. **How do you know the evidence was not altered?** Every raw record is stored verbatim with a SHA-256; the workflow ledger is a hash chain that detects edits, deletions and reordering (`correlation/ledger.py: verify_chain`). It is tamper-evident since submission, not immutable, and tail truncation is detected only against a trusted head. Show: `Panel_Viva_Walkthrough.md` section 3b, the tamper test in `test_multisource_workflow.py`.
12. **Is there real human approval?** For the multi-source report, yes: export returns 409 until the case lead approves the exact current result, and approval goes stale if evidence changes (`.../multisource/review`, `.../multisource/export`). For the case-synthesis report path, no. Detection-rule approval does not exist because Detection Engineering is design only (ADR-012). Show: `test_multisource_review.py`, `test_multisource_export_gate.py`.
13. **How secure is it?** Two reviews, neither a penetration test. The earlier OWASP-mapped probe suite (33 offline cases, 18 of 18 live). Then, on 2026-11-22, a review of every module against the OWASP API Security Top 10 (2023): 14 defects fixed, each with a failing test written first (invalid-key lockout with Retry-After, rate limits that random-key rotation cannot dodge, 1 MiB caps on small write endpoints, security headers and no-store on every response including 429s, production refusing the default database password and a wildcard CORS setting, a readiness page that no longer leaks internals, keys redacted from logs, a cap on concurrent model calls). The object-level access check (IDOR) was run live: 3 outsider roles against 18 case routes returned the same response for a case that exists and one that does not. Most fixes are verified by offline tests; the live server was probed before the fixes, and afterwards only the new headers and the invalid-key response were re-checked. Open and disclosed: the evidence pool is shared by design (single-tenant, ADR-014 approved), no HTTP key-rotation endpoint, throttles are per process, API key held in browser session storage, two dev-server dependency advisories (vite, esbuild) not upgraded, hosted database password to be rotated after the defence. Show: `docs/07_Security/OWASP_API_Top10_Review.md`, `docs/07_Security/OWASP_Top10_2021_Checklist.md`. Do not say "pentested".
14. **Why a 1.5B local model, and is it any good?** It fits a 4 GB GPU and keeps data local. It is small and its answers are short and less nuanced. Today I found and fixed a real fault that made it look worse than it is: the prompt is about 5,400 tokens, and Ollama silently cut it to about a quarter at the window I had set, so the model never saw its instructions. With a window that holds the whole prompt the model cites real events and the report no longer falls back. The provider now refuses an answer from a truncated prompt, and the pipeline falls back to the deterministic report with the cause logged. A larger model (`qwen3:4b`) answered correctly but took 174 s on this GPU. I have not benchmarked model quality (0 of 19 live benchmark scenarios captured, and the live numbers above are one case). Show: `docs/06_AI/AI_Latency.md`, `backend/tests/unit/test_ollama_truncation_guard.py`.
15. **What would you do next, and what is out of scope?** First: label the 67 development cases, score B0, B1 and AISOP, then the held-out split once, and report results including failures. Then the literature and product review. Detection Engineering (Sigma rules, never auto-deployed) is designed in ADR-012 but not built. GRC and compliance workflows are out of scope. Show: `Evaluation.md` "Next steps".
